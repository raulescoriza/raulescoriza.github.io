The problem

Exercise apps list movements by body part. Clinicians decide whether, where to start and when to progress.

What most apps do

  • Pick “shoulder”, get a generic list of exercises.
  • No check for signs that need a doctor first.
  • Same starting point for an irritable shoulder and a mildly stiff one.
  • Progression by calendar, or not at all.

My role in this project

  • Clinical logic: screening, irritability classification, dosage and progression criteria, from my practice as a physiotherapist.
  • Product & UX design: flow, screens and visual design in Figma.
  • Implementation: a React prototype of the shoulder flow is next, built AI-assisted.
How it works

From a body region to a safe, graded progression

1

Pick a region

Tap the area on a front or back body map. Regions follow anatomy — knee, hip, lumbar spine — not vague labels like “leg”.

2

Describe what you feel

Filter by symptom, irritability and recovery phase. Answers that suggest a red flag stop the flow and point you to a professional.

3

Follow a graded progression

Get a short set with dosage, clear cues and the criteria to move up a level — or step back.

Design decisions · Shoulder

From a tap on the shoulder to a safe, graded progression

How the app decides whether it is safe to show exercises, where to start, and when to progress. The app does not diagnose: it screens, classifies irritability and doses load.

Step Decision Stop / refer Outcome Exercise level
Step 1

User taps the shoulder (left or right)

Front or back view. The region is anatomical, not a vague “arm”.

Step 2 · Decision

Red-flag screen — 5 yes/no questions

Trauma with deformity or inability to lift the arm · pain with exertion plus chest pain, breathlessness or cold sweat · fever or a red, hot, swollen joint · cancer history, unexplained weight loss or constant night pain · tingling down the arm or progressive weakness.

Safety comes before engagement. Screening runs before any exercise is visible, even though it adds friction to the first session.
Any “yes” → stop

Show a referral, not exercises

Trauma → A&E · chest symptoms → emergency number · fever / hot joint → urgent doctor · cancer, weight loss, night pain → GP · neurological signs → doctor. Each answer maps to its own urgency level and copy.

All “no”
Step 3 · Decision

Out-of-scope check

Recent shoulder surgery? Marked loss of movement even when someone else moves the arm, especially external rotation?

Out of scope is not the same as dangerous. These users get a calm “see a physio in person” message, with different tone and urgency than a red flag.
Recent surgery

Follow the surgeon’s protocol

The app does not override post-operative rehab plans.

Marked passive range-of-motion loss

Recommend in-person assessment

Possible stiff / frozen shoulder: exercise selection needs a hands-on exam.

Neither
Step 4 · Decision

How irritable is the shoulder?

Three questions: pain at rest · waking at night · how long pain lasts after activity (hours / under 1 h / minutes).

The starting level depends on how reactive the shoulder is, not on how ambitious the user is.
High irritability

Start at Level 1

Moderate irritability

Start at Level 2

Low irritability

Start at Level 2 · Level 3 if tolerated

Level 1 · Isometrics

Wall external rotation · wall abduction

5 × 30 s at submaximal effort, no more than mild discomfort. Daily.

Level 2 · Light load

Band external rotation · band row · scaption to 90°

3 × 12–15, every other day.

Level 3 · Progressive load

Heavier band or weights · light overhead press · incline push-up

3 × 6–8, every other day.

Weekly check-in
Step 5 · Decision

Symptom response over the last ~2 weeks

Pain during exercise · pain the next morning compared with baseline · all sets completed without shrugging the shoulder.

Progress by symptom response, not by calendar. The same rule works for every level, so the logic stays explainable.
≤ 3–4/10 during, back to baseline next morning, full sets

Level up

Pain rises a little but recovers within 24 h

Stay at this level

> 5/10 during, or worse next morning in 2 sessions in a row

Level down

Exit

Leave the flow and refer

A red flag appears at any point, or there is no improvement after ~6 weeks → in-person assessment.

Clinical basis

Pain thresholds (≤ 3–4/10 during exercise, back to baseline within 24 h) are the criteria I used in clinical practice, in line with pain-monitoring approaches to loading.

Durations (~2 weeks to progress, ~6 weeks before referring) are approximate clinical estimates for a prototype, not a validated protocol.
